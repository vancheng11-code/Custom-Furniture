

Color consistency can be challenging when furniture contains multiple materials or is produced across several batches.

Wood, paint, fabric, metal, and other surfaces may each show color differences under different lighting conditions.

The safest approach is to establish an approved color reference before production.

For painted furniture, the exact color specification and finishing process should be documented.

For wood products, natural variation should be considered when defining acceptable differences.

Fabric and leather may also require approved swatches.

During production, quality-control teams can compare products against the approved reference rather than relying only on memory or photographs.

Lighting conditions should remain consistent during visual evaluation whenever possible.

Clear color standards can reduce disputes between buyers and manufacturers and help maintain brand consistency across multiple orders.
